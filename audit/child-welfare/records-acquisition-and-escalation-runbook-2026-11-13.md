# Phase F — Records Acquisition and Escalation Runbook

**Prepared:** 2026-11-13  
**Status:** Execution plan; no request has been submitted.  
**Purpose:** Obtain existing, non-identifying records sufficient to test whether Texas foster-care missing-child episodes were reported, actively investigated, tracked through recovery, and properly closed.

## 1. Non-negotiable safeguards

- Do not publish child names, dates of birth, addresses, schools, photographs, placement locations, medical information, case numbers, or case-level location clues.
- Do not attempt to access DFPS, law-enforcement, NCIC, TLETS, NCMEC, court, or provider systems without explicit lawful authorization.
- Use aggregate records, public reports, redacted administrative records, and de-identified extracts released by the record holder.
- Do not infer a child is still missing from a stale report, an unmatched total, or an administrative status. Current welfare checks and location work belong to authorized agencies.
- Preserve adverse, exculpatory, and inconclusive evidence. Obtain agency explanations before alleging misconduct.

## 2. Correct statutory control baseline

Texas Family Code §264.123 requires DFPS, for a child missing from substitute care while in managing conservatorship, to:
- Notify the appropriate law-enforcement agencies, court, attorney ad litem, guardian ad litem, and parent (subject to statutory exceptions) within 24 hours after learning the child is missing, or as soon as possible if a person cannot be notified within that period.
- Notify those parties of the child's return within 24 hours after DFPS learns of the return, subject to the same timing qualification.
- Continue locating efforts until the child returns to substitute care, including monthly contacts with law enforcement, relatives, former caregivers, and any state or local social-service agency that may be providing services.
- Conduct quarterly supervisory-level case reviews for children age **15 or younger**.
- Document locating actions, persuasion efforts, supervisory review discussions and determinations, and post-return discussions.
- Interview a returned child about the reasons for the absence, where the child stayed, and specified trafficking-related victimization, except where DFPS knows at return that the child was abducted and another agency is investigating. A qualifying disclosure must be reported to law enforcement within 24 hours.

Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Art. 63.00905 separately imposes duties on law enforcement after receiving a missing-child report, including immediate investigation and specified entries within two hours. Art. 63.0091 establishes enhanced response procedures for certain repeat-missing children, including foster-care/DFPS children reported missing at least twice in the preceding 24 months. Do not attribute law-enforcement duties to DFPS or vice versa.

Primary references:
- Texas Family Code §264.123: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/FA/htm/FA.264.htm
- Texas Code of Criminal Procedure Chapter 63: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CR/htm/CR.63.htm
- DFPS Missing Children Protocol (November 2025): https://www.dfps.texas.gov/handbooks/CPS/Resource_Guides/Missing_Children_Protocol.pdf

## 3. Acquire records in separate, narrow requests

The current DFPS request template is a broad inventory. To reduce delay and avoid asking an agency to create a new analysis, submit staged requests for existing records. Ask for clarification/narrowing discussions rather than allowing an overbroad request to stall.

### Request A — DFPS annual totals and cohort reconciliation (highest priority)

Ask DFPS for existing records underlying the published FY2023 and FY2024 missing-child totals, plus FY2025 totals if published/maintained:
1. The source tables, worksheets, report extracts, and definitions used to produce the annual missing-child and recovery totals.
2. Existing aggregate counts for unique children and missing episodes, repeat episodes, start-of-year missing status, end-of-year missing status, recovery during the year, and exit from conservatorship while missing.
3. The underlying definitions and cohort rules explaining the FY2023 figures (1,164 missing at some point; 1,016 located by August 31, 2023; 109 still missing in conservatorship at that date; 39 exits while missing; 1,095 recovered during FY2023).
4. The underlying definitions and cohort rules explaining the FY2024 figures (935 went missing; 919 recovered; 192 suspected/unconfirmed and/or confirmed trafficking).
5. Existing data dictionaries, field definitions, report-generation specifications, and QA/reconciliation documents.
6. If a requested breakdown is not maintained, state that the record does not exist; do not create a new dataset solely to answer the request.

### Request B — DFPS statutory compliance and records-management controls

Ask for the current and historical versions (FY2023–FY2025) of:
1. Missing-child protocols, job aids, forms, supervisor checklists, training materials, and statewide implementation notices.
2. Existing aggregate compliance reports or QA results for §264.123 notifications, monthly contacts, quarterly reviews for children age 15 or younger, documentation, recovery interviews, and trafficking-related referrals.
3. Existing audit-log policies, retention schedules, role/access controls, and system procedures for creating, editing, merging, closing, or deleting missing-episode records.
4. Existing guidance for a child who remains missing when conservatorship is terminated or the child turns 18; approval criteria, legal review procedures, and aggregate counts of petitions/orders while missing.
5. Existing DFPS communications and reconciliation records with law enforcement, DPS, NCMEC, courts, and providers about missing-child reporting.

### Request C — Texas DPS / law-enforcement implementation

Submit a separate request to the Texas Department of Public Safety for existing, non-identifying records:
1. Rules, implementation guidance, bulletins, training, and system workflow documents for Code of Criminal Procedure Arts. 63.00905 and 63.0091.
2. Existing aggregate measures of timeliness/compliance for TLETS/clearinghouse, NCIC (where releasable in aggregate), NCMEC entries, and 48-hour regional notifications.
3. Existing high-risk/repeat-missing designation procedures and aggregate counts of foster-care cases meeting the two-or-more-reports-in-24-months criterion.
4. Existing audit/QA findings, system exception reports, corrective-action records, and interagency implementation correspondence.
5. Existing data dictionaries and definitions for any released aggregate tables.

Do not request NCIC access or operational case records. Do not request confidential child identifiers.

### Request D — Local law-enforcement validation (targeted after aggregate response)

After identifying which jurisdictions hold the relevant records, submit targeted requests to selected local agencies for existing aggregate or redacted administrative records showing:
- date/time a missing-child report was received;
- date/time required system/NCMEC entries were made, if recorded in releasable form;
- repeat-missing escalation process and aggregate compliance;
- policy, training, and QA records.

Do not request an operational missing-child roster or publish any case-level details. Any case-level validation must be handled by authorized reviewers under appropriate confidentiality protections.

## 4. Submission and proof of receipt

The Texas Attorney General explains that a Public Information Act request must be in writing and delivered to the governmental body's public information officer (or designee) using a method permitted by law and accepted by that body. Permitted methods include U.S. mail, email, hand delivery, or another method approved by the governmental body.

Before sending:
- Confirm the current DFPS and DPS public-information submission instructions on each agency's official website.
- Replace the fee ceiling placeholder with an amount the requestor actually authorizes.
- Include the requestor's real contact details; do not invent or expose them in this public repository.
- Keep a timestamped copy of the exact request and proof of delivery/receipt.
- Ask for electronic delivery and CSV/XLSX/PDF where those formats already exist.
- If the agency says the scope is too broad, request a written narrowing proposal and prioritize Request A before B–D.

Official guidance: https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/open-government/members-public/how-request-public-information

## 5. Response tracking and challenge ladder

Create a private tracking log for each request with: agency, delivery method, sent timestamp, proof of receipt, statutory response dates, correspondence, fee estimate, records received, withholding basis, and next action. Do not store confidential child records in the public repository.

If a governmental body withholds records or does not respond:
1. Preserve the original request, proof of receipt, all correspondence, and the agency's stated legal basis.
2. Check whether the agency timely requested an Attorney General decision where required. The OAG explains that failure to respond, failure to provide information, or failure to request an OAG ruling within 10 business days may support a written complaint to its Open Records Division.
3. If the agency invokes an exception, ask for the precise statutory exception, the records or portions covered, and release of reasonably segregable nonconfidential material. Do not assume all child-welfare administrative records are public; confidentiality laws may protect child-level information.
4. If the agency fails to comply with an OAG ruling or appears to overcharge, use the OAG's stated complaint process and observe the stated deadline for overcharge complaints.
5. For a disputed withholding or a potential judicial challenge, obtain advice from a Texas public-information attorney before filing suit. Do not treat an OAG hotline response as legal representation or a court ruling.

Official OAG complaint guidance: https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/open-government/members-public/how-report-violation-public-information-act  
Open Government Hotline: (512) 478-6736; toll-free (877) 673-6839.

Important: The OAG's electronic filing portal for open-records decisions is not the portal for making an initial request to DFPS or DPS. Initial requests go directly to the governmental body that holds the records.

## 6. Federal oversight follow-up

Separately prepare FOIA requests to HHS/ACF and HHS OIG for existing federal records concerning:

- evidence supporting closure of recommendation 23-A-07-049.01;
- federal oversight and reporting guidance on missing-from-care episodes and NCMEC reporting;
- public work-plan records and eventual report materials for the HHS OIG Mississippi review.

Do not send the Texas Public Information Act request to HHS. Federal FOIA and Texas PIA are separate processes.

## 7. Audit acceptance criteria

Do not label the audit complete until:
- cohort definitions distinguish unique children from missing episodes;
- year-end unresolved status and exits while missing reconcile;
- report-receipt and system-entry timestamps can be compared against the correct legal deadlines;
- required monthly contacts and age-qualified quarterly reviews are tested;
- return interviews and trafficking-related disclosures/referrals are accounted for;
- repeat missing episodes and record edits/deletions are tested;
- contradictory and missing data are disclosed;
- DFPS and relevant law-enforcement agencies have had a fair opportunity to respond.
