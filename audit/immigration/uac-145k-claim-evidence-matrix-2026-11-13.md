# ICE/DHS 145,000 UAC Location Claim — Evidence Matrix

**Audit date:** 2026-11-13  
**Status:** Public-source review; underlying agency calculation not yet produced.  
**Scope:** Aggregate and program-level evidence only. No child identities, sponsor identities/addresses, location histories, or active investigative details.

## Executive finding

On February 10, 2026, ICE Senior Official Performing the Duties of the Director Todd M. Lyons told the House Committee on Homeland Security that DHS law-enforcement agencies and HHS had located more than 145,000 unaccompanied alien children (UACs) since February 2025. The same prepared statement cited more than 71,000 field engagements, more than 500 sponsor arrests, and more than 2,400 human-smuggling operations dismantled, including nearly 730 criminal prosecutions.

The hearing record supplies no calculation workpaper, source cohort, unique-child methodology, deduplication rule, operational definition of "located," child-contact rate, safety-assessment rate, or disposition breakdown. The claim is therefore **officially asserted but not independently validated by the public material reviewed**. This is an evidence gap, not proof that the number is false.

## Correcting the cited OIG statistic

The February 10, 2026 ICE testimony says the August 2024 DHS OIG report concluded that more than 291,000 UACs were "simply lost" by the system.

That is not what OIG-24-46 says. The August 2024 management alert states that as of May 2024 ICE had not served Notices to Appear on more than 291,000 UACs. It separately reports more than 32,000 missed court appearances over FY2019–FY2023 and says ICE could not account for the location of all UACs released by HHS who did not appear.

The final OIG-25-21 report later states:
- 448,000+ UACs transferred from ICE to HHS during FY2019–FY2023;
- 31,000+ release addresses blank, undeliverable, or missing apartment numbers;
- 233,000+ UACs without served NTAs as of January 2025; and
- 43,000+ UACs served NTAs who failed to appear, as reported by ICE as of October 2024.

These are different measures, dates, and populations. They cannot be added, subtracted, or treated as equivalent without a documented cohort crosswalk.

## Priority production request

Request existing records supporting the testimony, not a new agency-created analysis:

1. Frozen dashboard extracts and exact calculation workpapers used for the 145,000 and 71,000 figures.
2. Written definitions, source cohort, inclusion/exclusion dates, and deduplication rules.
3. Unique-child counts by month, field office, source cohort, and disposition.
4. Direct child contact separated from sponsor-only, household-only, address-only, school-record-only, and unsuccessful contacts.
5. Counts of safety assessments, trafficking screenings, child-protection/law-enforcement referrals, ORR re-entry, reunification, other placements, and unresolved status.
6. Substantiation standards and counts distinguishing allegations, referrals, investigations, confirmed victims, charges, and convictions.
7. Aggregate crosswalks with OIG-24-46/OIG-25-21 cohorts, ORR records, NTA service, and missed-hearing data.
8. Written questions for the record and agency responses associated with the February 10, 2026 House hearing.
9. Evidence supporting later statements that children were "returned" or "reunified" with families/loved ones, distinct from location confirmation.
10. OIG recommendation 1 closeout evidence and the current ICE/HHS information-sharing agreement.

## Interpretation rules

- "Located" is not automatically "directly contacted."
- "Directly contacted" is not automatically "safety assessed."
- "Safety assessed" is not automatically "safe outcome verified."
- A referral is not proof that the receiving entity accepted or investigated it.
- An arrest is not a conviction.
- An investigation or allegation is not a substantiated finding.
- An engagement is an event, not a unique child.
- Never identify children or sponsors in public audit artifacts.
