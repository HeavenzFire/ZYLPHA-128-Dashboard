## A. ICE / DHS — HSI and ERO records supporting the 145,000 located claim

**Submit through:** ICE SecureRelease portal. Choose Department of Homeland Security → U.S. Immigration & Customs Enforcement (ICE). ICE's FOIA office says program/operational requests should describe the records, date range, and likely office; FOIA does not require the agency to create a new analysis.

**Suggested subject:** Records supporting ICE/DHS February 10, 2026 testimony on unaccompanied children located, field engagements, sponsor arrests, and trafficking investigations

**Request text:**

Under the Freedom of Information Act, 5 U.S.C. § 552, I request electronic copies of existing agency records created or maintained from February 1, 2025 through the date this request is processed, concerning ICE/HSI efforts to locate unaccompanied children previously transferred to HHS/ORR or released to sponsors.

This request specifically seeks the existing records supporting statements made by ICE senior official Todd M. Lyons in testimony before the House Committee on Homeland Security on February 10, 2026, including statements that DHS/HSI and HHS had located more than 145,000 unaccompanied children since February 2025 and had conducted more than 71,000 field engagements.

Please provide:

1. The definitions, methodology, counting instructions, data dictionaries, source-system descriptions, reporting dashboards, and calculation workpapers used to produce the 145,000+ located-children figure and the 71,000+ field-engagement figure.
2. Existing aggregate tables or reports showing counts by month, field office/region, activity type, outcome category, and source cohort. Please identify whether each count represents a unique child, a unique case, a household, a visit, an attempted contact, or another event.
3. Existing deduplication rules and quality-control/reconciliation procedures used to avoid counting one child more than once across field engagements, offices, agencies, or reporting periods.
4. Existing aggregate crosswalks, if any, linking these efforts to the DHS OIG OIG-25-21 FY2019–FY2023 transfer cohort; children without NTAs; children with NTAs who failed to appear; and children whose addresses were incomplete or undeliverable.
5. Existing aggregate outcome records showing how children counted as located were classified, including direct contact with the child, sponsor/household-only contact, address verification, referral to child protection or law enforcement, safety assessment, reunification, continued placement, new placement, return to ORR care, other government custody, adulthood, departure, detention, removal, or unresolved status.
6. Existing policies, standard operating procedures, field guidance, tasking memoranda, training materials, and after-action reports governing child location efforts, direct child safety checks, sponsor verification, and referral/escalation of suspected trafficking, abuse, neglect, or forced labor.
7. Existing aggregate records and methodology supporting the February 10, 2026 testimony that ICE arrested more than 500 criminal sponsors and dismantled more than 2,400 human-smuggling operations since February 2025. Please distinguish arrests, charges, indictments, convictions, and operation counts, and indicate overlap with the children counted as located.
8. Existing interagency agreements, data-sharing protocols, and aggregate referral/reconciliation reports between ICE/HSI, ICE/ERO, HHS/ORR, DOJ/EOIR, DOL, FBI, and state/local partners concerning this population.
9. Existing records showing implementation and validation of DHS OIG recommendations in OIG-24-46 and OIG-25-21, including management decision letters, closure packages, evidence submitted to OIG, and OIG validation or follow-up correspondence.

I do not seek personally identifying information about any child, sponsor, household member, or witness, nor details that could expose a child or compromise an active investigation. Please withhold or redact protected fields and release all reasonably segregable non-exempt portions. Aggregate tables, redacted logs, methodology, data dictionaries, and program-level records are preferred.

If any portion is withheld, please identify the specific FOIA exemption and release all reasonably segregable material. If the request is considered too broad, please contact me before closing it so that it can be narrowed to the records supporting the 145,000 and 71,000 figures.

**Fee request:** I request a public-interest fee waiver because disclosure will contribute to public understanding of federal child-welfare, immigration case-tracking, and anti-trafficking operations and is not sought for commercial use. ## B. HHS / ACF / ORR — sponsor safety and National Call Center records

**Submit through:** https://www.hhs.gov/foia/request/index.html (HHS directs new FOIA requests to FOIA.gov; ACF is handled by the HHS Office of the Secretary FOIA Program).

**Suggested subject:** ORR post-release child safety follow-up, National Call Center, sponsor vetting, and referral outcomes

**Request text:**

Under FOIA, 5 U.S.C. § 552, I request electronic copies of existing HHS/ACF/ORR records created or maintained from July 1, 2024 through the date this request is processed concerning post-release safety follow-up for unaccompanied children released to sponsors.

Please provide:

1. The current ORR National Call Center contract, statement of work, performance standards, quality-assurance requirements, and records documenting the transition to the new contractor on December 15, 2025, including acceptance testing and continuity controls.
2. Existing monthly aggregate reports, dashboards, and data extracts for July 2024 to present showing the number of unique reports, unique children represented, inability-to-contact reports, runaway reports, abuse/neglect allegations, trafficking concerns, sponsor-child conflicts, child-protection referrals, law-enforcement referrals, NCMEC referrals, Office on Trafficking in Persons referrals, successful direct child contacts, follow-up contacts, and documented case dispositions.
3. Existing data dictionaries, definitions, coding manuals, and deduplication rules used for the categories above, including the distinction between a report, an incident, an episode, and a unique child.
4. Current Safety and Well-Being Follow-Up Call policies, call schedules, exception criteria, documentation requirements, escalation pathways, and aggregate quality-assurance/audit results.
5. Current sponsor screening policies and procedures, including required identity, FBI fingerprint, state child-abuse registry checks, mandatory and discretionary home studies, pending-check handling, sponsor household-member screening, and post-release updates.
6. Existing aggregate reports on cases in which required checks were not documented, checks remained pending after release, home studies were required but not completed, sponsor information changed, or a child could not be contacted.
7. Existing policies, data fields, audit logs, and aggregate reports concerning sponsor changes of address, child moves, runaway episodes, reports of children no longer residing with sponsors, and referrals to CPS, law enforcement, NCMEC, or trafficking-response entities.
8. Existing records documenting HHS's reconciliation of ORR post-release safety data with ICE/HSI location efforts and DOJ/EOIR immigration case data, including data-sharing agreements and aggregate reconciliation reports.
9. Existing HHS management responses and implementation evidence for HHS OIG report OEI-07-24-00370 and the 2024 sponsor-screening/follow-up review.

I do not seek child-level personal information, sponsor identities or addresses, shelter locations, or active investigative details. Please provide aggregate tables and redacted program records and withhold only protected portions, releasing all reasonably segregable material.

If HHS believes a requested item is maintained by another component, please identify the component or refer the relevant portion as permitted. If the request is too broad, please contact me to narrow it rather than administratively closing it.

**Fee request:** I request a public-interest fee waiver because disclosure will contribute to public understanding of the operation and performance of a federal child-safety program and is not sought for commercial use. Please notify me before incurring fees above **[REQUESTER'S AUTHORIZED FEE LIMIT]**.

## D. Supplemental ICE initiative records — UAC Joint Initiative / Field Implementation

A publicly accessible copy of a four-page ICE document titled **"Unaccompanied Alien Children Joint Initiative — Field Implementation"**, document identifier **2025-ICFO-22246-000001 through 000004**, describes the February 2025 initiative. The copy is marked "For Official Use Only / Law Enforcement Sensitive" and is hosted by the Center for Progressive Reform. Treat it as a lead; request the official releasable record and any subsequent revisions directly from ICE.

Public copy: https://wp-cpr.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/2025/05/ICE-HSI-Cooperation-2025-Unaccompanied-Children.pdf

The document identifies a multi-source UAC dashboard, a daily field-contact documentation form, HSI/ERO coordination, and a leadership progress dashboard. The FOIA request should seek the releasable program-level records, not child-specific operational target packets or live investigative information.

**Supplemental request language to add to Request A:**

Please include records associated with the ICE "Unaccompanied Alien Children Joint Initiative — Field Implementation" (document identifier 2025-ICFO-22246), including:

1. The final approved version, all releasable revisions, superseding guidance, implementation memoranda, and records identifying the responsible ICE components and offices.
2. The data dictionary, population definitions, source-system inventory, cohort selection criteria, deduplication rules, and quality-control procedures for the UAC Power BI application or successor dashboard used by this initiative.
3. The field-contact documentation form schema/data dictionary, blank form template, completion instructions, and aggregate monthly counts of records submitted, unique children represented, repeat contacts, attempted contacts, successful direct contacts, sponsor-only contacts, address verifications, and unresolved outcomes.
4. Aggregate monthly and cumulative initiative outcome reports, including the definitions used for "located," "contacted," "field engagement," and "welfare check"; the number of unique children in each category; and the documented disposition categories.
5. Existing records showing how initiative cases were reconciled with ORR UAC Portal records, HHS National Call Center safety reports, EOIR case data, and the OIG OIG-25-21 cohort. Aggregate or redacted crosswalk documentation only.
6. Existing oversight, audit, quality assurance, and supervisory review records testing whether a "located" designation reflected direct child contact and a documented safety assessment, as distinct from a door knock, sponsor contact, or address verification.
7. Existing closeout, transfer, or follow-up procedures for children whose status remained unknown after an attempted field engagement.

I do not request target packets, child-level records, sponsor identities or addresses, location histories, facial-recognition queries, investigative leads, undercover methods, or details that would compromise active law-enforcement operations. Please provide only program-level methodology, blank templates, aggregate statistics, and reasonably segregable records.
