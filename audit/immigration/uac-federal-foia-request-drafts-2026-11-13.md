## A. ICE / DHS — HSI and ERO records supporting the 145,000 located claim

**Submit through:** ICE SecureRelease portal. Choose Department of Homeland Security → U.S. Immigration & Customs Enforcement (ICE). ICE's FOIA office says program/operational requests should describe the records, date range, and likely office; FOIA does not require the agency to create a new analysis.

**Suggested subject:** Records supporting ICE/DHS February 10, 2026 testimony on unaccompanied children located, field engagements, sponsor arrests, and trafficking investigations

**Request text:**

Under the Freedom of Information Act, 5 U.S.C. § 552, I request electronic copies of existing agency records created or maintained from February 1, 2025 through the date this request is processed, concerning ICE/HSI efforts to locate unaccompanied children previously transferred to HHS/ORR or released to sponsors.

This request specifically seeks the existing records supporting statements made by ICE senior official Todd M. Lyons in testimony before the House Committee on Homeland Security on February 10, 2026, including statements that DHS/HSI and HHS had located more than 145,000 unaccompanied children since February 2025 and had conducted more than 71,000 field engagements.

Please provide:

1. The definitions, methodology, counting instructions, data dictionaries, source-system descriptions, reporting dashboards, and calculation workpapers used to produce the 145,000+ located-children figure and the 71,000+ field-engagement figure.
2. Existing aggregate tables or reports showing counts by month, field office/region, activity type, outcome category, and source cohort. Please identify whether each count represents a unique child, a unique case, a household, a visit, an attempted contact, or another event.
3. Existing deduplication rules and quality-control/reconciliation procedures used to avoid counting one child more than once across field engagements, offices, agencies, or reporting periods.
4. Existing aggregate crosswalks, if any, linking these efforts to the DHS OIG OIG-25-21 FY2019–FY2023 transfer cohort; children without NTAs; children with NTAs who failed to appear; and children whose addresses were incomplete or undeliverable.
5. Existing aggregate outcome records showing how children counted as located were classified, including direct contact with the child, sponsor/household-only contact, address verification, referral to child protection or law enforcement, safety assessment, reunification, continued placement, new placement, return to ORR care, other government custody, adulthood, departure, detention, removal, or unresolved status.
6. Existing policies, standard operating procedures, field guidance, tasking memoranda, training materials, and after-action reports governing child location efforts, direct child safety checks, sponsor verification, and referral/escalation of suspected trafficking, abuse, neglect, or forced labor.
7. Existing aggregate records and methodology supporting the February 10, 2026 testimony that ICE arrested more than 500 criminal sponsors and dismantled more than 2,400 human-smuggling operations since February 2025. Please distinguish arrests, charges, indictments, convictions, and operation counts, and indicate overlap with the children counted as located.
8. Existing interagency agreements, data-sharing protocols, and aggregate referral/reconciliation reports between ICE/HSI, ICE/ERO, HHS/ORR, DOJ/EOIR, DOL, FBI, and state/local partners concerning this population.
9. Existing records showing implementation and validation of DHS OIG recommendations in OIG-24-46 and OIG-25-21, including management decision letters, closure packages, evidence submitted to OIG, and OIG validation or follow-up correspondence.

I do not seek personally identifying information about any child, sponsor, household member, or witness, nor details that could expose a child or compromise an active investigation. Please withhold or redact protected fields and release all reasonably segregable non-exempt portions. Aggregate tables, redacted logs, methodology, data dictionaries, and program-level records are preferred.

If any portion is withheld, please identify the specific FOIA exemption and release all reasonably segregable material. If the request is considered too broad, please contact me before closing it so that it can be narrowed to the records supporting the 145,000 and 71,000 figures.
