# Phase E — VA Community Care dental reimbursement finding

**Assessment date:** 2026-11-13  
**Primary report:** VA OIG Audit 23-00748-28, issued 2025-02-20  
**Finding class:** verified control failure and excessive reimbursement; not a fraud finding  
**Primary sources:** [VA OIG report page](https://www.vaoig.gov/reports/audit/community-care-network-outpatient-claim-payments-mostly-followed-contract-rates-and), [full 56-page audit PDF](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-03/VAOIG%2023-00748-28.pdf), [Oversight.gov report record](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/community-care-network-outpatient-claim-payments-mostly-followed-contract-rates-and)

## Executive result

A specific, quantified federal payment-control failure is established by the Department of Veterans Affairs Office of Inspector General (VA OIG). In its February 20, 2025 audit of the Veterans Health Administration's Community Care Network (CCN), the OIG found that VHA paid Optum approximately **$783.4 million more** and TriWest approximately **$127.3 million more** than the TPAs paid community dental providers for services from FY2020 through May 2024. The total difference was approximately **$910.7 million**.

The strongest discrete causal finding is a contracting-officer modification error affecting Optum's regions 1–3. A modification intended to add prices for new dental procedure codes mistakenly incorporated a new fee schedule for existing codes as well. The OIG calculated that this increased payments by **$648,662,846** from June 2021 through May 2024. The replacement schedule for existing codes averaged approximately **66% above** the original contracted rates. The OIG states that contracting officers acknowledged the mistake after the OIG brought the modification to their attention. (PDF pp. 25–26; Appendix C, p. 41.)

This is the audit's strongest current “smoking gun”: a primary-source-documented contract modification error with a quantified payment effect, a named contracting office, a defined period, a recipient, and an OIG finding. It is evidence of an administrative/control failure and excessive reimbursement. **It does not, by itself, establish bribery, kickbacks, intentional fraud, collusion, or criminal conduct.**

## What the OIG established

### 1. Dental reimbursement differential — $910.7 million

- Optum (CCN regions 1–3): VHA paid about **$783.4 million** more than Optum paid community dental providers.
- TriWest (CCN region 4): VHA paid about **$127.3 million** more than TriWest paid community dental providers.
- Period: FY2020 through May 2024.
- The audit analyzed the full dental-claims universe for CCN regions 1–5 through May 2024 rather than a sample for this dental comparison (PDF pp. 3–4 and 23–26).
- Contracts for regions 1–4 lacked the dental pass-through language present in region 5. That meant TPAs could seek reimbursement from VHA above what they paid providers, up to contracted rates. (PDF pp. ii–iii, 23–24.)
- The report identifies this as excessive dental reimbursement. Do not equate the entire $910.7 million automatically with cash recoverable from the TPAs: the contract terms, actual provider payments, and any subsequent recovery/settlement must be reconciled.

### 2. Specific Optum contract modification error — $648,662,846

- In March 2021, Optum submitted a schedule containing both new and existing dental procedure codes.
- The intended modification was to establish rates for new codes only.
- Strategic Acquisition Center contracting officers mistakenly incorporated the proposed rates for existing codes too.
- The new existing-code rates averaged approximately 66% above the original contracted rates.
- The OIG's region-by-region table reports:
  - Region 1: $164,629,465
  - Region 2: $121,888,941
  - Region 3: $362,144,440
  - Total: **$648,662,846**
- The modification language and independent government cost estimate did not indicate that existing rates were to be raised. A contracting officer confirmed the change should have applied only to new codes. Contracting staff said they had not realized the existing rates increased until the OIG identified it.
- A May 2023 modification restored existing-code rates to the original contracted rates, but the OIG reported that Optum continued to seek and receive reimbursement at the increased rates as of August 2024.
- As of August 2024, VA offices were still quantifying the impact and pursuing recovery. (PDF pp. 25–26.)

### 3. Separate outpatient payment-rate overpayments — estimated $178.5 million

The OIG separately estimated $105.1 million in Optum overpayments and $73.4 million in TriWest overpayments for outpatient healthcare claims due to incorrect Medicare/VA fee schedules and related controls. These amounts are statistical projections based on samples, not a full census. The audit reported 90% confidence intervals in Appendix B (PDF pp. 37–39). Keep these distinct from the $910.7 million dental reimbursement differential; do not add them into a single “loss” figure without respecting the OIG's separate monetary-benefit classifications.

## Monetary-benefit classification and source discrepancy

The report's Appendix C (PDF p. 41) classifies:
- **$980.3 million** as funds for better use (including $910.7 million for dental reimbursement controls and $69.6 million for other rate controls).
- **$108.9 million** as questioned costs related to identifying and recovering contract overpayments.
- **$0** of the $108.9 million as unsupported costs.

The current VA OIG report page repeats $108.9 million questioned costs and $980.3 million funds for better use, and its recommendation rollup reports $910.7 million monetary impact still open and $178.5 million closed.

However, the Oversight.gov report-level record currently displays $0 questioned costs and $0 funds for better use, despite linking to the same VA OIG report. This is a source-system metadata conflict. The report PDF Appendix C and VA OIG's own report page support the non-zero values; preserve both records and flag Oversight.gov's summary metadata as inconsistent rather than silently choosing or averaging them.

That closure status does **not** disclose the amount recovered. Do not report the $648.7 million or $910.7 million as recovered, nor as still entirely outstanding, without a recovery ledger or closure documentation specifying amounts.

## Evidence classification

- **Verified finding:** OIG-reported $910.7 million dental reimbursement differential; OIG-attributed $648,662,846 Optum modification effect; underlying period, contract mechanism, and region-level arithmetic.
- **Verified finding:** estimated $178.5 million outpatient payment-rate overpayments, with sampling/90% confidence intervals.
- **Corroborated status snapshot:** VA OIG page lists #3 and #5 open and five other recommendations closed/implemented as captured 2026-11-13.
- **Disputed metadata:** VA OIG report page/PDF report $108.9m questioned costs and $980.3m funds for better use; Oversight.gov report-level metadata reports zero for both.
- **Not established:** actual amount recovered from Optum; whether any official knowingly caused or concealed the modification error; fraud, kickbacks, bribery, conspiracy, or criminal intent.
